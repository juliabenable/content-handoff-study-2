Rights tab: what to build

A new Rights tab on the campaign page, next to Dashboard and Content. The brand downloads each creator's video, sees when its usage rights end, and offers the creator more time or paid use. Everything on this page is decided by Julia (and Tony on Oct 2). The prototype is the spec for every screen and every string; this page adds the rules behind them and what the backend needs.

The rules

In words

  1. Starting rights. A field on the brand (or its campaign): how many days and which type. The clock starts the day the post goes live. The end date is a live day: on Feb 24 a video whose rights end Feb 24 shows "today" under its end date, and download still works.
  2. Only posted videos. A video joins the Rights tab the day its post goes live. Not posted yet means not on the tab.
  3. Types. Organic only ("Use it on your own channels.") or Organic + paid ("Also run it as a paid ad."). Never paid alone.
  4. Lengths. 90 days, 1 year, Perpetual. 1 year ends on the same date the next year. Perpetual has no end date.
  5. More time follows on. If the creator says yes before the current end date, the new period starts at that end date. After the end date, it starts the day the creator says yes.
  6. Adding paid starts at the yes. If paid use is already running, more paid follows on from where it ends. Organic rights with no end date carry on after the paid period.
  7. Paid on top of organic with no end date. Such a video can still take an offer, organic + paid only (Organic only is greyed: "Already yours, with no end date."). In a sheet that mixes such a video with others, Organic only is off for the whole offer ("Not in an offer with these videos."), since an offer asks for the same rights on each video. Once paid use has no end date too, there is nothing left to buy.
  8. Nothing is erased. What the brand holds stays in force while a new offer is out, and after a no.
  9. Paid added over organic days already bought. The new paid term covers those days; they are not pushed later, and the end date does not move. Organic rights that end after the new paid term, or have no end date, run on after it. The price is the full organic + paid price, nothing off.
  10. Per creator. Extend on any of a creator's videos opens all of that creator's videos that can take an offer. The brand cannot leave one out. One price for the set, one answer for the set. A video the creator posts after the offer went out joins the next offer to that creator, never a separate one.
  11. Ending soon is the last 7 days of the rights, or the last 7 days of paid use when organic rights run on after it. A row with an offer out or a counter waiting shows the offer instead.
  12. Download works while the rights are live and stops when they end; an extension brings it back.

As pseudo-code

// per brand (or campaign): Simple { days: 90, type: organic+paid }, basic plan { days: 30, type: organic }
periods = [{ start: posted_on, end: posted_on + base.days, type: base.type }]

for each accepted offer (a yes, or an accepted counter), in order:
  Y = accepted_on; last = periods.last
  if last.end == none and not (offer.type == organic+paid and last.type == organic): skip   // nothing follows rights with no end date
  if offer.type == organic+paid and (last.end == none or Y <= last.end):
    from = (paid running at Y) ? end of that paid use : Y
    held = last                                   // what the brand held before this yes
    cut every period at from
    add { start: from, end: from + term, type: organic+paid }
    if term has an end and (held.end == none or held.end > from + term):
      add { start: from + term, end: held.end, type: held.type }   // it runs on after
  else if Y <= last.end:  add { start: last.end, end: last.end + term, type }  // follows on
  else:                 add { start: Y, end: Y + term, type }               // after the end

term: 90 days | 1 year (same date next year) | Perpetual (end = none)
ends        = periods.last.end            // none = "No end date"
type today  = the period live today; on a boundary day paid wins
ended       = today > ends               // checked first; download off
ending soon = 0 <= days to end <= 7, or 0 <= days to paid end <= 7 and organic runs on

Prices

The offer prefills with the price for the type and length; the brand can type its own amount. The line under the "Your offer" label, above the amount, says "For 90 days of organic + paid rights on both of Quinn's videos, we suggest $75. Quinn can say no or ask for more." The price is for the creator's whole set.

Organic + paid$75$150$200
Organic only$50$100$150

Adding paid on top of organic with no end date uses the organic + paid row, nothing off.

What the sheet opens on. 90 days, and the type the videos hold now. When their types differ, or a video's organic rights have no end date, it opens on organic + paid. "Current" marks the type the videos hold now; it is hidden when any of them has ended or they hold different types. Changing type or length refills the suggested price. Closing and reopening the same creator's sheet keeps the draft: type, length, amount and note. A sheet for one video opens with what the brand holds ("You have 3 more days of organic + paid rights, until Feb 27."); a sheet for 2 or more videos shows them as small pictures, each with its end date under it.

What the brand owes

The backend keeps track of the total each brand owes for extensions (Julia, Oct 6).

  • One line per accepted offer: brand, campaign, creator, the videos it covers, type of rights, length, the amount offered, the counter if there was one, the agreed amount, the day it was accepted.
  • A creator's yes owes the brand's offer. An accepted counter-offer owes the counter. Offers sent, declined or not answered owe nothing.
  • A creator's set counts once. One offer covers all of a creator's videos at one price. If the price is stored on each video's record, summing per video counts a set twice; sum per offer.
  • The total per brand and per campaign is the sum of agreed amounts. The rights a contract already includes (Simple's first 90 days) are not in it.

In the prototype, Feb 24: Quinn $75 for 2 videos plus Sofia $150 = $225.

The 7-day email

  • In the first ship. A daily job. Brands already get scheduled emails (draft review, campaign availability), so it can follow the same pattern.
  • Who gets it: only brands with paid rights. One contact per brand; at Simple, Katherine.
  • When: 7 days before a creator's rights end, or 7 days before paid use ends when organic rights run on after it. The end date includes any accepted extension, so an extended video is reminded 7 days before its new end.
  • One email per creator. When a creator's videos end on different days, one email goes out 7 days before the first end, listing each video and its end date.
  • A button, Open the Rights tab, links to the campaign's Rights tab.
Quinn's videos: usage rights start ending Feb 16

Not in this build

  • A creator app or screen for offers. Katie's team relays offers and answers to creators for now.
  • Instant approval when a creator has already named a price, and pricing by creator size. Agreed for a later version.
  • Ad codes (Spark, partnership codes), which a client asked for on Sep 18. No field collects them yet; closed by Julia on Oct 6.
  • A rights calendar. Dropped by Tony on Oct 2.
  • Rules for what a basic-plan brand must stop doing at day 30. The tab tells brands nothing about it.
  • A library across campaigns, and a Benable fee on extensions. Closed by Julia.
  • No video popup. Every action is on the row.
  • Moving money for extensions. This build tracks what each brand owes (see What the brand owes); paying the creator and invoicing the brand are not part of it.

Demo only: do not build

  • The black demo bar: the day switch (Dec 10, Feb 24, Apr 2), the creator's Yes / Counter / No, the reset, and the "BRAND OWES" readout.
  • The example data (Simple, the creators, their seeded offers and answers), and a counter being $25 more than the offer.
  • Messages that start with "Demo:". They stop the demo from rewriting a day that comes later than the one on screen; a real calendar only moves forward.
